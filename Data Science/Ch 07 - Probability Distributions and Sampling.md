## Probability Distributions

Probability distributions describe the likelihood of different outcomes in a random experiment. They include discrete and continuous probability distributions.

### Discrete Probability Distributions

Discrete probability distributions describe the probability of discrete outcomes. They include the Bernoulli distribution, binomial distribution, Poisson distribution, and geometric distribution.

#### Bernoulli Distribution

The Bernoulli distribution describes the probability of a single binary outcome. It is defined by a single parameter p, which is the probability of success.

#### Binomial Distribution

The binomial distribution describes the probability of a fixed number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials. It is defined by two parameters n and p, where n is the number of trials and p is the probability of success.

#### Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution describes the probability of a fixed number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space. It is defined by a single parameter λ, which is the average number of events.

#### Geometric Distribution

The geometric distribution describes the probability of the number of trials until the first success in a series of independent Bernoulli trials. It is defined by a single parameter p, which is the probability of success.

### Continuous Probability Distributions

Continuous probability distributions describe the probability of continuous outcomes. They include the normal distribution, uniform distribution, exponential distribution, and beta distribution.

#### Normal Distribution

The normal distribution describes the probability of continuous outcomes that are symmetrically distributed around the mean. It is defined by two parameters μ and σ, where μ is the mean and σ is the standard deviation.

#### Uniform Distribution

The uniform distribution describes the probability of continuous outcomes that are equally likely to occur within a fixed interval. It is defined by two parameters a and b, where a is the lower bound and b is the upper bound.

#### Exponential Distribution

The exponential distribution describes the probability of continuous outcomes that are the time between events in a Poisson process. It is defined by a single parameter λ, which is the average number of events.

#### Beta Distribution

The beta distribution describes the probability of continuous outcomes that are the probability of success in a Bernoulli trial. It is defined by two parameters α and β, where α is the number of successes and β is the number of failures.

## Sampling

Sampling is the process of selecting a subset of data from a larger dataset. It includes random sampling, stratified sampling, and sampling bias.

### Random Sampling

Random sampling involves selecting a subset of data randomly from the larger dataset. It ensures that each data point has an equal chance of being selected.

### Stratified Sampling

Stratified sampling involves dividing the dataset into homogeneous subgroups (strata) and then randomly sampling from each subgroup. It ensures that each subgroup is represented in the sample.

### Sampling Bias

Sampling bias occurs when the sample is not representative of the larger dataset. It can occur due to various reasons, such as non-random sampling, selection bias, and response bias.

### Central Limit Theorem

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) states that the sampling distribution of the mean of any independent, identically distributed random variables will be normal, regardless of the underlying distribution. It is a fundamental concept in statistics and probability.

## Conclusion

Probability distributions and sampling are crucial concepts in data science. By understanding the different types of probability distributions and sampling techniques, we can ensure the accuracy and reliability of our analysis and insights.
